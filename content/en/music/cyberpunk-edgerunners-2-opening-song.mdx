Night City has a new anthem, and it's older than the city itself. The **Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 opening song** is "10:15 Saturday Night" by The Cure, confirmed on July 9, 2026, alongside an ending theme from French singer-songwriter Petite Meller.

Why does that matter? Because music has never been background noise in this franchise. The first season's soundtrack outlived the season itself, turning a ten-episode story into a permanent part of the wider Cyberpunk 2077 experience. The **Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 opening song** carries that same weight now: it has to introduce a new director, a new cast, and a story that has nothing to do with David Martinez.

Here's everything we know about the theme songs, who made them, and why a 1979 post-punk track might be the smartest creative choice the production team could have made.

## The Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 Opening Song: The Cure's "10:15 Saturday Night"

The reveal came through the show's official social channels on July 9, 2026, in a short "preview shard" post that framed the two tracks as the first pieces of a larger soundtrack rollout. The post paired the opening credits track with the ending credits track and promised the rest of the music would arrive alongside the series itself.

"10:15 Saturday Night" is one of the earliest songs in The Cure's catalog. It originally appeared on the band's 1979 debut album *Three Imaginary Boys*, and it belongs to the band's lean, angular post-punk era — long before the lush, gothic soundscapes they became famous for in the mid-1980s. That distinction matters when you start thinking about how the track will be used.

Petite Meller's "Watson" fills the ending credits slot. Meller is a French singer-songwriter known for a stylized, retro-tinged pop aesthetic, which makes for an interesting tonal counterweight to a sparse English post-punk opener.

## Why The Cure Fits Night City Better Than You'd Expect

On paper, a 1979 English post-punk single sounds like an odd match for a neon-drenched cyberpunk anime. In practice, it's close to perfect — and the reason comes down to restraint.

"10:15 Saturday Night" is a song about waiting. It's built on a repetitive, almost mechanical bass figure, minimal drumming, and a vocal that sounds bored and lonely at the same time. There's no catharsis in it. It just sits there, ticking. That's a very Night City emotion: the feeling of being surrounded by millions of people and still ending up alone on a Saturday night.

The season's official story description reinforces that read. It follows Weak, a washed-up cyberpunk legend forced to live without chrome, searching for purpose in a world that has moved past him, alongside D, a nomad on a revenge path that drags him toward corporate secrets. A song about quiet, unresolved waiting fits both characters better than a triumphant anthem would.

Community reports since the reveal suggest the announcement landed as a genuine surprise — not because The Cure is an unusual pick for anime in general, but because the first season leaned so heavily on modern, propulsive electronic and rock tracks. The pivot is deliberate.

## What We Know About the Rest of the Season 2 Soundtrack

Beyond the two theme songs, the biggest music news is the composer change. Tsuneo Imahori takes over scoring duties for the new season, stepping into a role that Akira Yamaoka filled for the first series.

Imahori is best known for his work on *Trigun*, where his guitar-driven, genre-blurring approach gave that show a distinct identity that didn't sound like standard anime orchestration. If he brings even a fraction of that texture to Night City, the season's background score could sound noticeably different from what fans remember.

The announcement post explicitly teased that more soundtrack details would follow closer to release. Based on how the first season handled its music — layering licensed tracks into key story beats rather than saving them for credits — it's reasonable to expect additional artist announcements in the months ahead.

## How to Get the Most Out of the Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 Opening Song

If you want to be ready for the premiere, a little prep work goes a long way. Here's a practical approach:

- **Listen to the original track first.** Pull up "10:15 Saturday Night" on your streaming service of choice and pay attention to the bass line. It's the spine of the song, and it's almost certainly what an anime arrangement would build around.
- **Listen to the full album.** *Three Imaginary Boys* is short and worth hearing in order. It gives you context for what The Cure sounded like before their sound got bigger and gloomier.
- **Compare it to "This Fyre."** Franz Ferdinand's Season 1 opener was fast, brash, and instantly energizing. Hearing the two back to back tells you a lot about the tonal shift the new season is going for.
- **Check out Petite Meller's catalog.** Her retro-pop sensibility is a different flavor entirely, and knowing both tracks gives you a fuller picture of the season's emotional range.
- **Follow the official channels.** The reveal post promised more soundtrack news, so the official *Cyberpunk: Edgerunners* account is the fastest place to catch additional artist announcements.

For reference, the full theme song announcement is available through [Crunchyroll's news coverage of the Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2 theme songs](https://www.crunchyroll.com/news/latest/2026/7/9/cyberpunk-edgerunners-2-anime-theme-songs), which includes the original reveal post from the show's social team.

One thing worth managing expectations on: an anime opening theme is often a shortened, re-arranged edit of the original track. The version you hear over the credits may not match the 1979 recording note for note. That's normal, and it's usually where a good sound director earns their paycheck.
