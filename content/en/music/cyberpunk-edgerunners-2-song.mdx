Netflix's return to Night City finally has its soundtrack, and it is a bold one. On July 9, 2026, the Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 song lineup was officially revealed: The Cure's "10:15 Saturday Night" will open the series, while Petite Meller's "Watson" will play over the ending credits. If you remember how deeply music shaped the first season's emotional gut punch, this Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 song announcement is the clearest signal yet of what the new ten-episode story is chasing.

Why does it matter? Because in this franchise, theme songs are not decoration. They are worldbuilding. The first season turned a Franz Ferdinand track and a Dawid Podsiadło ballad into permanent residents of Night City's mental skyline, and fans still argue about which one hit harder. With a new director, a new cast, and a standalone story, the Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 song picks are the first real taste of the sequel's personality — and they suggest a colder, stranger, more melancholy ride than before.

## The Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 Song Lineup: Opening and Ending Themes

The reveal came via the official Cyberpunk: Edgerunners social accounts and was quickly covered by [Crunchyroll News' announcement of the Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2 theme songs](https://www.crunchyroll.com/news/latest/2026/7/9/cyberpunk-edgerunners-2-anime-theme-songs). Two tracks, two very different moods, one shared obsession with atmosphere.

| Track | Artist | Role | Musical Lane |
|---|---|---|---|
| "10:15 Saturday Night" | The Cure | Opening theme | English post-punk / new wave |
| "Watson" | Petite Meller | Ending theme | French indie pop |

The Cure need no introduction. The English rock band helped define the brooding, reverb-soaked sound that a lot of cyberpunk-adjacent media borrows from, and "10:15 Saturday Night" comes from their early catalog — lean, anxious, and built on a hypnotic guitar figure. It is the kind of song that sounds like waiting for something bad to happen in a rented apartment while the city hums outside.

Petite Meller is a French singer-songwriter with a fondness for bright, slightly surreal pop. Pairing her with The Cure gives the second season a deliberate contrast: a cold, ticking opening and a warmer, more disorienting ending. That push-and-pull is exactly the shape a Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 song pairing needs when the story is about people who have been left behind by the world they helped build.

## Why The Cure and Petite Meller Fit Night City's Sound

Night City has always been scored like a memory rather than a place. The original series leaned on propulsive rock for its action beats and a slow, devastating ballad for its final gut punch. The new pairing keeps that structure but shifts the emotional register.

Here is what each pick brings to the table:

| Element | What The Cure Brings | What Petite Meller Brings |
|---|---|---|
| Tempo | Steady, hypnotic, almost mechanical | Light, danceable, off-kilter |
| Mood | Anxiety, isolation, cold nostalgia | Whimsy with an unsettling edge |
| Narrative fit | The grind of a city that never stops | The strange tenderness between broken people |
| Fan expectation | Instant recognition for older viewers | A discovery moment for newer fans |

Think about the story the show is telling. A washed-up legend named Weak is learning to live without chrome, while a nomad named D chases revenge into corporate secrets that were never meant to surface. That is a story about obsolescence and obsession — and a 1970s post-punk track about staring at the ceiling at 10:15 on a Saturday night is a shockingly precise match for a man who has been rendered obsolete by his own city.

Meanwhile, an ending theme is where a show exhales. If "Watson" carries the dreamlike, slightly absurd energy Petite Meller is known for, it could give each episode a soft landing that makes the next one hurt more.

## Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 Songs vs. Season 1: What Changed

Fans inevitably compare. Season 1's music became inseparable from its legacy, so the sequel's choices are being measured against two songs that already achieved iconic status.

| Category | Season 1 | Season 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Opening theme | Franz Ferdinand, "This Fffire" | The Cure, "10:15 Saturday Night" |
| Ending theme | Dawid Podsiadło, "Let You Down" | Petite Meller, "Watson" |
| Era of source tracks | Contemporary rock | Classic post-punk plus modern indie pop |
| Director | Hiroyuki Imaishi | Kai Ikarashi |
| Composer | — | Tsuneo Imahori |
| Story format | David's rise and fall | Standalone ten-episode arc |

The most interesting shift is the era. Season 1's opening was a modern, adrenaline-forward rock track, which matched David's hunger. The sequel reaches back decades for its opener, which fits a protagonist who is literally living in the past. That is not a downgrade — it is characterization through licensing.

There is also a structural change worth noting. Season 1 wove its ending song into a single, unforgettable finale moment. A standalone series with a fresh cast has more freedom to let its ending theme function as a recurring ritual rather than a one-time payoff. If the Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 song choices are deployed that way, viewers may end up associating "Watson" with ten separate emotional beats instead of one.
