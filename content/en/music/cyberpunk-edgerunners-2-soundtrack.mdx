### Opening Theme: "10:15 Saturday Night" by The Cure

The Cure need no introduction. Formed in Crawley, England, in the late 1970s, the band built a career on exactly the mood this franchise trades in: rain-slick melancholy, restless guitar lines, and a voice that sounds like it's been up all night.

"10:15 Saturday Night" is one of their earliest songs, originally from their 1979 debut album. It's a slower, brooding piece rather than an obvious anthem, which makes it a genuinely interesting choice for an opening credits sequence. If you've watched the first season, you know that this series has never used its OP as background noise — the opening is part of the storytelling.

### Ending Theme: "Watson" by Petite Meller

Petite Meller is a French singer-songwriter known for a dreamy, jazz-tinged take on pop — sweet on the surface, slightly strange underneath. That tonal mismatch is precisely why she fits.

The title is worth noting too. Watson is one of the most recognizable districts in Night City as it appears in *Cyberpunk 2077*, a neon-soaked commercial zone that fans of the game know well. Whether the song is literally about the district or just borrows its name is unclear, but it signals that the music team is thinking in-universe rather than treating the soundtrack as a generic playlist.

## Who Is Composing the Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 Soundtrack Score?

The biggest piece of news for score nerds: Tsuneo Imahori is handling the music. Imahori is best known for his work on *Trigun*, where his guitar-forward, jazz-rock and blues-inflected cues gave a sci-fi western an instantly recognizable identity.

What does an Imahori score actually sound like in practice? Expect live-feeling guitars, walking bass lines, and brass stabs rather than wall-to-wall synth pads. That's a meaningful shift for a franchise whose visual language is aggressively electronic. A jazz-rock undercurrent can make a firefight feel sweaty and human instead of sterile — and this season's story, about a legend forced to live without chrome, is nothing if not human.

## Season 1 vs. Season 2: How the Music Direction Changed

Comparing the two seasons side by side makes the creative pivot obvious. Season 1 leaned into a specific blend: licensed rock for the OP, an emotionally devastating pop ballad for the ED, and a score that mixed industrial textures with piano-driven grief.

| Element | Season 1 | Season 2 |
|---|---|---|

| Composer | Akira Yamaoka | Tsuneo Imahori |
| Director | Hiroyuki Imaishi | Kai Ikarashi |

Three things stand out:

- **Both seasons open with a real-world rock band.** Season 1 tapped Franz Ferdinand; Season 2 goes older, colder, and more gothic with The Cure. It's the same strategy — borrow a recognizable band's credibility — executed with a very different emotional temperature.
- **Both seasons close with a European solo artist.** Dawid Podsiadło's ending theme became one of the most enduring parts of Season 1's legacy, so bringing in Petite Meller suggests the team wants another slow-burn closer that fans will still be humming months later.
- **The score is being handed to a different voice entirely.** Akira Yamaoka's work is defined by dread and atmosphere. Imahori's is defined by motion and muscle. That's a tonal shift you'll hear in the first action scene.
