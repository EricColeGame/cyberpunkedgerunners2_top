Naming an ending theme after a neighborhood rather than a character or an emotion suggests the show wants you thinking about place as much as people.

## Why The Cure's "10:15 Saturday Night" Fits the Opening Slot

On paper, a 47-year-old album track might seem like an odd choice for a series built on chrome, corpo warfare, and hyper-violence. In practice, it's close to perfect.

"10:15 Saturday Night" is sparse, patient, and deeply lonely. It's a song about waiting — sitting in a room, listening to a tap drip, watching time pass. The first season of *Edgerunners* opened with Franz Ferdinand's propulsive "This Fffire," which announced itself like a fist through a window. The Cure's track does the opposite: it lowers you into the world before anything explodes.

That tonal shift lines up almost exactly with what we know about the story. The official synopsis describes "Weak," a washed-up cyberpunk legend forced to live without chrome while searching for purpose in a world that's moved past him. A slow, melancholy song about waiting alone in a room is a remarkably literal match for that character.

| Song Element | Musical Trait | Story Parallel |
|---|---|---|
| Tempo | Slow, restrained | Reflects a legend running out of momentum |
| Lyrics | Isolation, waiting, quiet dread | Matches "Weak" living without chrome |
| Production | Minimal, cold, spacious | Mirrors Night City's empty late-night streets |
| Era | Late-1970s post-punk | Echoes the series' retro-futurist aesthetic |

There's also a nice thematic loop here. The first season drew heavily on British post-punk and new wave for its needle drops, and leaning on an actual foundational post-punk band for Season 2 feels like the series acknowledging its own lineage.

## Petite Meller's "Watson" and the Art of the Ending Theme

Ending themes in anime carry a different job than openings. An opening sets the promise; an ending sits with the aftermath. Season 1's "Let You Down" by Dawid Podsiadło became inseparable from the show's emotional gut punches, and it's still one of the most-streamed anime ending tracks of the decade.

Petite Meller is an interesting counterweight. The French singer-songwriter built her reputation on a sound that blends vintage pop arrangements with modern electronic textures — dreamy, slightly off-kilter, and visually theatrical. That aesthetic overlaps neatly with *Cyberpunk*'s core contradiction: the world looks like a retro-futurist fantasy, but the lived reality underneath is brutal.

A new track titled "Watson" gives her room to do something specific. It doesn't have to be a cover, a remix, or a callback. It can be written directly for the show, which means it can speak to the season's themes — family, obsession, legacy — without needing to borrow weight from an existing song.

Community reports so far are mostly first-impression reactions rather than analysis, but the shared read is consistent: the pairing of a somber opening and a dreamlike ending suggests a slower, more reflective season than the first.

## How the Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 Theme Song Compares to Season 1

Placing the two seasons side by side makes the creative intent easier to read. Season 1's music was loud, fast, and confrontational. Season 2's first announced tracks are quieter and more introspective.

| Element | Season 1 | Season 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Opening theme | "This Fffire" — Franz Ferdinand | "10:15 Saturday Night" — The Cure |
| Ending theme | "Let You Down" — Dawid Podsiadło | "Watson" — Petite Meller |
| Music composer | Akira Yamaoka, with Marcin Przybyłowicz | Tsuneo Imahori |
| Director | Hiroyuki Imaishi | Kai Ikarashi |
| Studio | TRIGGER | TRIGGER |
| Story | Original cast, standalone arc | Standalone arc with new leads |

Both seasons share the same studio and the same universe, but the shift in musical leadership is significant. Hiroyuki Imaishi's Season 1 was defined by maximalist energy — *Promare* levels of visual and sonic overload. Kai Ikarashi steps into the director's chair for Season 2, and Ikarashi's background as an animation director on *Little Witch Academia* suggests a more controlled, character-focused approach.

That doesn't mean the show will be slow. It means the chaos will be punctuated differently.

## Tsuneo Imahori Takes Over the Score

Behind the two licensed songs sits the original score, and that job now belongs to Tsuneo Imahori. Anime fans will recognize his name immediately from *Trigun* — a series whose soundtrack blended blues, jazz, and desert-rock guitar into something unmistakable.

That's a fascinating fit. *Trigun* is, at its core, a story about a legend burdened by his own reputation, wandering a hostile world while trying to hold on to a personal code. *Edgerunners 2* is described in strikingly similar terms: a fallen legend, a revenge-driven nomad, and a world that has already decided who these people are.

| Role | Name | Notable Credits |

The combination of Imahori's guitar-forward scoring and The Cure's cold post-punk opener suggests a soundtrack with real texture — one that moves between dust, neon, and silence rather than sitting in a single lane.

## FAQ

**What is the Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 theme song for the opening credits?**

The opening theme is "10:15 Saturday Night" by The Cure, the English post-punk band's 1979 track from their debut album *Three Imaginary Boys*. It was confirmed by the official *Cyberpunk: Edgerunners* account on July 9, 2026.

**Who performs the ending theme?**

French singer-songwriter Petite Meller performs "Watson," a new track written for the series. The title references the Watson district of Night City.

**When does the Cyberpunk Edgerunners 2 theme song release in full?**

The full soundtrack, including the remaining tracks, arrives alongside the series when it premieres on Netflix in Fall 2026. The two lead tracks were announced ahead of the release.

**Do I need to watch Season 1 first?**

No. Season 2 is a standalone ten-episode story following new characters — "Weak," a former cyberpunk legend living without chrome, and "D," a nomad on a revenge path. Watching Season 1 is recommended for tone and context, but it isn't required.

**Why did the music style change between seasons?**

The production team changed. Season 1 was directed by Hiroyuki Imaishi with music by Akira Yamaoka and Marcin Przybyłowicz. Season 2 is directed by Kai Ikarashi with music by Tsuneo Imahori, and the shift toward a quieter, more reflective opener reflects that new creative leadership.
