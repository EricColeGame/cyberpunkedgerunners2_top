## How "Watson" Fits Night City's Evolving Sound

Season 1's music worked because it refused to stay in one lane. Franz Ferdinand's "This Fffire" gave the opening a jittery, adrenalized edge, while "Let You Down" closed episodes with slow-burn devastation. The pairing made the show feel like a mixtape someone built inside a burning city.

Season 2 appears to be following that blueprint with a different palette. The Cure brings a colder, more melancholy kind of rock — a sound that pairs naturally with a story about a legend who has been left behind by the world. Petite Meller, meanwhile, is known for a stylized art-pop sensibility, which suggests the ending credits may lean dreamlike rather than purely mournful.

Here's what a great ending theme has to accomplish in a show like this:

- **Land the emotional payoff.** The last ninety seconds of an episode decide what you remember.
- **Contrast the opening.** High-energy intros hit harder when the outro pulls the air out of the room.
- **Work outside the show.** "Let You Down" became a standalone hit because it holds up without visuals.
- **Reward rewatching.** Lyrics that read as romantic on episode one often read as tragic by episode ten.

If "Watson" pulls off even two of those, it will earn its place in the fandom's playlists fast.
